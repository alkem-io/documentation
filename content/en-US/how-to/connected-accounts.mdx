# Connect or disconnect a sign-in method

Your account can have more than one way to sign in: a password, a passkey, and
any identity provider Alkemio supports — for example **CleverBase**. The
**Connected Accounts** section in your security settings shows every way you can
currently sign in, and lets you connect a new provider or disconnect one you no
longer use.

Connecting a provider does not create a second account and does not change your
profile. It adds another door into the account you already have.

## Where to find it

1. Click on your profile picture in the top right corner.
2. Go to **Settings** > **Security**.
3. **Connected Accounts** is the first section on the page.

## What the section shows

- **A row for each identity provider** Alkemio supports, marked **Connected** or
  **Not connected**, with a **Connect** or **Disconnect** button.
- **A row for Password and one for Passkey**, marked **Set** or **Not set**.
  These are shown for completeness only — they are managed in their own sections
  further down the page, and the **Change password** and **Manage passkeys**
  links take you straight there.

A connected provider shows its name and nothing else. Alkemio does not display
the name, email or any other detail the provider holds about you.

## Connect a provider

1. In **Connected Accounts**, find the provider you want and click **Connect**.
2. You are taken to the provider to confirm it is you.
3. When you come back, the provider is marked **Connected** and you can use it to
   sign in from then on.

If you last signed in a while ago, Alkemio first asks you to confirm it is you.
Do that, and your request continues on its own — you do not have to start over.

Your display name, profile and everything else on your account stay exactly as
they were.

## Disconnect a provider

1. In **Connected Accounts**, find the connected provider and click
   **Disconnect**.
2. Confirm in the dialog that appears.

You can no longer sign in with that provider. You can connect it again later,
but if the provider has changed how it identifies you in the meantime, that same
connection may not come back. Disconnecting never requires you to visit the
provider — so if a connection has stopped working, you can always remove it and
make a fresh one.

## Your last sign-in method is protected

You cannot disconnect the only way you have left to sign in. If a provider is
your last method, its **Disconnect** button explains why it is unavailable:

> This is your only way to sign in right now — add a password or a passkey below
> before you can disconnect it.

Set a password or register a passkey first, then come back and disconnect the
provider.

## If the identity belongs to another account

An identity can be connected to one Alkemio account at a time. If you try to
connect one that is already in use elsewhere, the attempt is refused and nothing
changes on either account:

> That identity is already connected to a different Alkemio account. Nothing has
> changed on either account.

Sign in to that other account and disconnect the identity there first.

## If something goes wrong

- **The attempt did not finish** — you abandoned it, it expired, or the provider
  refused it. You are returned to **Connected Accounts** with an explanation, and
  your ways of signing in are unchanged. Click **Connect** again to retry.
- **Alkemio cannot show your sign-in methods** — the section says so, offers
  **Try again**, and shows no provider as connected or not connected. No connect
  or disconnect action is offered until it can read your real state, so you are
  never acting on a guess.

## "I may already have an account"

If you sign **up** with a provider rather than signing in, Alkemio warns you
before you continue:

> You are trying to sign up with CleverBase. If you already have an Alkemio
> account with the same email, CleverBase will be connected to that account and
> you can use both sign-in methods from then on.

So if the email is the same, just continue — the provider is connected to your
existing account automatically, and there is nothing to clean up afterwards. Use
**Sign in with a different account** only when the provider carries a *different*
email from the account you already have; continuing would otherwise leave you
with a second, separate account.
